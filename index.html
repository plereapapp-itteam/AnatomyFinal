☠ Dangerous Relations

AORTIC ANEURYSM

PERICARDIUM

  • ⚡Thoracic outlet syndrome → lower brachial plexus/subclavian compression, sometimes from cervical rib.

Abdomen

TOS priority ≈ 10% / 10 items

★ Must-Know Structures

  • ★Abdominal cavity is continuous inferiorly with pelvic cavity; it has no true floor.
  • ★Greater sac ↔ lesser sac through epiploic/omental foramen.
  • ★Portal triad: bile duct + proper hepatic artery anterior; portal vein posterior.
  • ★Foregut = celiac trunk.
  • ★Midgut = SMA.
  • ★Hindgut = IMA.
  • ★Renal hilum anterior→posterior = Vein–Artery–Pelvis (VAP).
  • ★Diaphragm levels: IVC T8 | Esophagus T10 | Aorta T12.
  • ★Lumbar plexus L1–L4 forms within psoas major.
  • ★Abdominal wall nerves travel between internal oblique and transversus abdominis.

Pelvis and Perineum

TOS priority ≈ 10% / 10 items

★ Must-Know

  • ★Pudendal nerve = S2–S4.
  • ★Pelvic splanchnic nerves = S2–S4 parasympathetic.
  • ★Levator ani = puborectalis + pubococcygeus + iliococcygeus.
  • ★Pudendal nerve/internal pudendal vessels traverse Alcock canal.
  • ★Ischial spine is the key landmark for pudendal nerve block.
  • ★Pelvic TOS explicitly includes pelvic floor, pelvic viscera, fascia, superficial/deep perineal pouches, and male/female perineum.

Back and Spinal Column

TOS priority ≈ 10% / 10 items

★ Must-Know

  • ★Spinal cord ends approximately L1–L2 in adults.
  • ★Dural sac/subarachnoid space extends to approximately S2.
  • ★Lumbar puncture: L3–L4 or L4–L5.
  • ★Iliac-crest line approximates L4.
  • ★Deep intrinsic back muscles → posterior rami.
  • ★Anterior spinal artery supplies anterior two-thirds of spinal cord.
  • ★Paired posterior spinal arteries supply posterior cord.
  • ★Internal vertebral venous plexus is valveless and connects pelvis, abdomen, thorax, skull.

Visual Map

Cord ends around L1–L2; the dural sac continues to S2 — the safe LP window sits below the cord.

⚡ Root Lesions

LesionHigh-yield deficit
L4Weak knee extension; ↓ patellar reflex
L5Weak great-toe dorsiflexion
S1Weak plantar flexion; ↓ Achilles reflex
L4–L5 disc herniationUsually compresses L5 root
L5–S1 disc herniationUsually compresses S1 root

☠ Dangerous Relations

HIGH LP
Lumbar puncture above L2 risks direct cord injury.
AORTIC SURGERY
Thoracoabdominal aortic surgery may compromise artery of Adamkiewicz → spinal cord ischemia.
METASTASIS ROUTE
Valveless vertebral plexus permits pelvic cancer spread to spine.

⚡ Classic Syndromes

  • ⚡Anterior spinal artery syndrome → bilateral motor + pain/temp loss; dorsal columns relatively spared.
  • ⚡Cauda equina syndrome → saddle anesthesia + bladder/bowel dysfunction + LMN signs.

★ Surface / Compartment Landmarks

  • ★Femoral triangle: NAVEL lateral→medial = nerve, artery, vein, empty space, lymph.
  • ★Femoral nerve is outside femoral sheath.
  • ★Popliteal fossa: nerve → vein → artery, superficial→deep.
  • ★Anterior leg → deep fibular nerve.
  • ★Lateral leg → superficial fibular nerve.
  • ★Posterior leg → tibial nerve.

Blood & Lymph

  • ★Dorsalis pedis is continuation of anterior tibial artery.
  • ★Great saphenous vein → femoral vein.
  • ★Small saphenous vein → popliteal vein.
  • ★Most superficial lower-limb lymph → superficial inguinal nodes.

☠ Dangerous Relations

FIBULAR NECK
Common fibular nerve winds around fibular neck.
HIP DISLOCATION
Posterior hip dislocation can injure sciatic nerve.
FASCIAL COMPARTMENTS
Compartment syndrome threatens nerves/vessels within rigid fascial compartments.
KNEE DISLOCATION
Popliteal artery is deepest major structure in popliteal fossa and vulnerable in knee dislocation.

⚡ Classic Eponyms / Signs

  • ⚡Trendelenburg sign → superior gluteal nerve/gluteus medius-minimus dysfunction.
  • ⚡Foot drop → common fibular or deep fibular lesion.
  • ⚡Achilles reflex = S1, predominantly tibial nerve.
  • ⚡Patellar reflex = L3–L4, predominantly femoral nerve.

Skin and Appendages

TOS priority ≈ 10% / 10 items

★ Must-Know Layers

  • ★Epidermis → dermis → subcutaneous tissue → deep fascia.
  • ★Thick skin: palms/soles; has stratum lucidum, no hair follicles.
  • ★Epidermis is avascular.
  • ★Dermis contains vessels, nerves, lymphatics, and appendages.
  • ★Snell explicitly identifies epidermis/dermis and appendages including hair, nails, sebaceous glands, and sweat glands.

Final Board-Distractor Pairs

  • Direct hernia = medial; indirect = lateral to inferior epigastric vessels.
  • Femoral nerve = outside femoral sheath; femoral artery/vein = inside.
  • Phrenic = anterior to lung root; vagus = posterior.
  • LAD = anterior interventricular; posterior interventricular artery usually from RCA.
  • Type I pneumocyte = gas exchange; Type II = surfactant.
  • Bronchus = cartilage; bronchiole = no cartilage.
  • Duodenum = Brunner glands; ileum = Peyer patches.
  • Thyroid = follicles + colloid; parathyroid = chief cells, no follicles.
  • Thymus = Hassall corpuscles; lymph node = follicles/cortex-medulla.
  • DRG = pseudounipolar; autonomic ganglion = multipolar.
  • Skeletal muscle = peripheral nuclei; cardiac = central nuclei + intercalated discs.
  • Epidural = middle meningeal artery; subdural = bridging veins.
  • Axillary nerve = surgical neck; radial nerve = midshaft/radial groove; ulnar nerve = medial epicondyle.
  • Superior gluteal lesion = Trendelenburg; common fibular lesion = foot drop.
  • L4–L5 disc → L5 root; L5–S1 disc → S1 root.
  • Ureter under uterine artery; ovarian/testicular lymph → para-aortic, not superficial inguinal.
  • Carpal tunnel median lesion: lateral 3½ digits + thenar weakness, but thenar skin often spared.
  • CN XII LMN lesion: tongue points toward lesion.
  • CN X palatal lesion: uvula points away from lesion.
  • C3–C5 keeps the diaphragm alive.
